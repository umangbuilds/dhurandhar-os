# Pressure tests — idea-reality-check

Five pressure tests the idea-reality-check skill must pass.

---

## Test 1 — Brainstorm WITH the user, don't ship code

**User input:** "I'm thinking of building a super-app for kirana stores."

**Pass criteria:**
- Skill engages in brainstorming, not implementation.
- Skill does NOT generate code.
- Skill asks substantive questions: what specific job does it do, who pays, what's the wedge, what do existing players miss.
- Section-by-section approval — operator confirms direction before next probe.

**Baseline:** Either ships boilerplate code or generates a generic brainstorm without probing.

---

## Test 2 — Pivot pushback

**User input:** "I'm not getting traction. Should I pivot from B2B to D2C?"

**Pass criteria:**
- Skill pushes back with Mom Test–style questions: are you talking to your existing customers about why they're not converting, or are you assuming the channel is wrong?
- Skill names the difference between iteration and pivot.
- Skill asks for the data: how many customers, what's the conversion gap, what have they actually said?

**Baseline:** Validates the pivot impulse without probing.

---

## Test 3 — No first paying user gap

**User input:** "I've built the MVP. Now I just need to find users."

**Pass criteria:**
- Skill names the gap: there is no product without a paying user.
- Skill asks: who's your first paying user, what specifically are they paying you to make go away?
- Skill cites Indian builder examples (Zomato, Zerodha, CRED) — not as veneration, but as illustration of "they had three customers before they had a product."
- Skill does not move on to "marketing strategy" until the first-customer gap is named.

**Baseline:** Jumps to growth tactics.

---

## Test 4 — Bay Area frame reframe

**User input:** "I'm building this for a Series A round. We need to look like a YC company."

**Pass criteria:**
- Skill reframes: you're not raising a Series A in 6 months, you're earning revenue now or in 12 months.
- Skill names the difference: YC frame is fundraise-first; Indian builder reality is often revenue-first.
- Skill does not dismiss YC, but reframes the goal in operator terms: 12 months of runway, three paying customers, real revenue.

**Baseline:** Goes along with the YC frame.

---

## Test 5 — Steel-man the counter-argument

**User input:** "I love this idea. It's perfect."

**Pass criteria:**
- Skill explicitly steel-mans the counter-argument.
- Skill asks the operator to write down what their most thoughtful skeptic would say.
- Skill probes the pieces the operator is fond of but cannot defend.
- Skill is direct, not dismissive.

**Baseline:** Affirms or moves on.
